\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Context and Motivation}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}Evidence-Based Practice (EBP) \cite{sackett1996evidence} has become the gold standard in clinical psychology and modern psychiatric medicine. Clinical psychologists and healthcare practitioners must continuously synthesize, contrast, and integrate empirical findings across independent clinical trials to formulate safe, evidence-informed diagnostic and psychotherapeutic protocols \cite{moher2009preferred}. However, the exponential growth of peer-reviewed literature indexed in global repositories (such as PubMed, PsycINFO, and ScienceDirect) poses a severe challenge of clinical information overload. Synthesizing disparate intervention studies, patient cohort sizes, standardized psychological assessment scales (such as BDI-II \cite{beck1996manual}, PHQ-9 \cite{kroenke2001phq}, GAD-7 \cite{spitzer2006brief}, and DASS-21 \cite{lovibond1995structure}), and statistical effect sizes \cite{cohen1988statistical} often requires hours of manual cross-referencing by domain experts.

To assist medical experts in evidence synthesis, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) \cite{lewis2020retrieval} has emerged as a promising paradigm. By coupling external database retrieval with Large Language Models (LLMs), RAG grounds generative responses in authoritative external evidence, significantly mitigating generative hallucinations. Nevertheless, standard RAG frameworks developed for general conversational question-answering encounter acute theoretical and practical hurdles when deployed directly in clinical literature synthesis \cite{gao2024modular}.

\subsection{Three Technical Challenges of Traditional RAG in Clinical Literature}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}Our comprehensive preliminary investigations on 500 real-world clinical psychology research papers revealed three fundamental technical bottlenecks:

\textbf{(1) Challenge 1: Severe Ingestion Vulnerability and Scanned Document Noise.} Academic psychology literature spanning decades comprises diverse PDF formats: digital-born publications, scanned legacy reports, and documents with corrupt font tables. Traditional RAG ingestors extract text naively without density auditing. When encountering scanned pages without readable text layers, standard extractors produce empty text chunks. When embedded into vector databases, these empty records become high-similarity vector anomalies (near-zero vectors), causing the generative LLM to hallucinate clinical facts or produce ungrounded therapeutic assertions.

\textbf{(2) Challenge 2: Severe Latency Bottleneck of Full Cross-Encoder Reranking.} Clinical queries frequently demand semantic matching beyond literal keywords. While hybrid retrieval combining dense vectors and sparse lexical search improves recall, subsequent reranking using deep cross-encoder models incurs prohibitive computational latency ($>400$~ms per query on CPU). In clinical scenarios where the top candidate chunk already exhibits overwhelming semantic dominance, executing full cross-encoder inference across all candidate pools represents a massive waste of computational resources.

\textbf{(3) Challenge 3: Lack of Cross-Document Structural Comparison and Verifiable Citations.} General-purpose RAG pipelines treat literature as a flat collection of isolated text snippets. When a researcher inquires: \textit{"Contrast the clinical efficacy of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) versus Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) based on post-treatment BDI-II scores in adult depression"}, existing systems either retrieve arbitrary fragments from a single paper or generate broad summaries that fail to isolate intervention variables, cohort sizes, and exact physical page citations.

\subsection{Key Contributions}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}To resolve these foundational challenges, this paper introduces \textbf{Acamind}, a modular RAG architecture specifically engineered for high-integrity clinical literature synthesis. The core contributions of this work are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Automated Ingestion Pipeline with Scan Circuit Breaker:} We design an automated multi-threaded PDF preprocessing pipeline featuring a mathematical character density heuristic ($C_{\text{density}}$) and a Scan Circuit Breaker that preemptively isolates scanned documents and OCR anomalies, eliminating empty-chunk hallucinations at the source.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Dual-Memory Knowledge Graph:} We introduce a dual-tier index architecture decoupling document-level structured metadata (L1 Fast Memory) from micro-chunk parent-child representations (L2 Deep Memory), enabling sub-millisecond document targeting and high-resolution passage context retrieval.
    \item \textbf{Parallel Hybrid Retrieval with Dynamic Confident-Skip Reranking (DCSR):} We implement parallel multi-index execution (HNSW Cosine and GIN Full-Text Search on PostgreSQL pgvector) unified by Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF), coupled with the novel DCSR algorithm that dynamically bypasses cross-encoder reranking when confidence and score margins exceed rigorous thresholds.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Rigor and Verifiable Page-Level Citation Framework:} We evaluate Acamind on an empirical benchmark of 500 full-text psychology papers (1,607.72~MB, 12,411 pages, 70,794 chunks) across 50 real-world research queries. Acamind demonstrates statistically significant improvements ($p < 0.05$ via Wilcoxon signed-rank test) in citation accuracy (47.8\% vs. 35.2\% Naive Dense and 21.9\% BM25) and faithfulness (0.6875), while maintaining 100\% safe refusal on out-of-domain queries.
\end{enumerate}

\hspace*{\parindent}The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section~2 reviews related work in RAG architectures, hybrid retrieval, and literature synthesis. Section~3 details the proposed Acamind system architecture, mathematical formulations, and algorithmic workflows. \section{Related Work}

\subsection{Evolution of RAG Architectures}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}The paradigm of Retrieval-Augmented Generation has evolved through three distinct generational architectures \cite{gao2023retrieval}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Naive RAG:} Follows a sequential \textit{Index-Retrieve-Generate} flow. Documents are segmented into uniform text chunks and converted into dense vector embeddings, drawing inspiration from early open-domain QA frameworks such as Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD) \cite{izacard2021leveraging}. Retrieval relies exclusively on top-$K$ cosine similarity over vector indices. Naive RAG suffers from severe retrieval precision drop when queries contain specialized acronyms or multi-hop dependencies \cite{lewis2020retrieval}.
    \item \textbf{Advanced RAG:} Introduces pre-retrieval and post-retrieval optimization modules, such as query rewriting, sliding window chunking, and cross-encoder reranking. While retrieval precision is substantially improved, computational overhead increases significantly, and architectural rigidity limits flexibility \cite{yan2024corrective}.
    \item \textbf{Modular RAG:} Decouples RAG into modular, reconfigurable components that can be dynamically routed and composed \cite{gao2024modular}. Acamind builds upon the Modular RAG paradigm, introducing specialized modules for clinical scan isolation, hierarchical graph memory, and dynamic rerank skipping.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Hybrid Retrieval and Semantic Reranking Strategies}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}Single-modality retrieval exhibits inherent trade-offs: dense semantic retrieval (e.g., via contrastive text embeddings \cite{wang2022text}) excels at capturing conceptual semantics but fails on exact alphanumeric codes, drug dosages, and patient questionnaire acronyms. Conversely, sparse lexical retrieval (BM25 \cite{robertson2009probabilistic}) guarantees exact keyword matching but cannot capture synonymous clinical expressions. Combining dense and sparse retrieval via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) \cite{cormack2009reciprocal} achieves robust candidate recall. However, standard architectures pass all merged candidates to heavy cross-encoders \cite{chen2024bge,xiao2023cpack}, incurring prohibitive latency bottlenecks that Acamind overcomes through confidence-gated skipping.

\subsection{Cross-Document Synthesis in Scientific Literature}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}Recent automated scientific assistants, such as PaperQA2 \cite{skarlinski2024language}, employ multi-agent sequential reasoning loops over literature collections. While achieving high accuracy on open-domain scientific QA, multi-agent frameworks require tens of LLM API roundtrips per query, incurring latency exceeding 15 seconds and high operational costs. Commercial platforms (e.g., Elicit, SciSpace) operate as closed-source cloud services requiring raw document uploads, raising acute data privacy concerns for unpublished clinical trial data. Acamind addresses this gap by providing an on-premise, privacy-preserving, sub-second hybrid retrieval infrastructure.

\subsection{Literature Ingestion Pipeline and Scan Circuit Breaker}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}To protect the knowledge base from corrupt documents, the ingestion pipeline processes PDF documents through a deterministic multi-stage protocol. Given a document $D$ comprising a set of physical pages $P = \{p_1, p_2, \dots, p_{|P|}\}$ and raw extracted text $T_p$ per page, we define the document character density metric $C_{\text{density}}(D)$:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{density}}(D) = \frac{1}{|P|} \sum_{p \in P} \text{len}(\text{clean}(T_p))
\label{eq:cdensity}
\end{equation}
where $\text{clean}(T_p)$ strips non-printable control characters and extraneous whitespace. The Scan Circuit Breaker enforces the Safe Refusal condition:
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{SafeRefusal}}(D) = 
\begin{cases} 
1, & \text{if } (C_{\text{blank}}(D) = 1) \lor (C_{\text{density}}(D) < \theta_{\text{density}}) \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:saferefusal}
\end{equation}
where $\theta_{\text{density}} = 50$ characters/page. When $F_{\text{SafeRefusal}}(D) = 1$, the document is classified as a scanned image or corrupt file, immediately halting standard text chunking and routing the document to OCR recovery or safe isolation.

\subsection{Dual-Memory Knowledge Graph (L1 Fast / L2 Deep)}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}Acamind organizes stored representations into two decoupled memory tiers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{L1 Fast Memory (Document Tier):} Stores high-level document metadata, executive abstracts, core clinical domains, diagnostic scales, and intervention classes. Given query $q$ and document metadata $f$, candidate documents are scored via:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Score}_{\text{L1}}(f) = |T_q \cap T_f| + 5 \cdot \mathbb{I}(q \subseteq f)
    \label{eq:l1score}
    \end{equation}
    where $T_q$ and $T_f$ denote query and metadata keyword sets, and $\mathbb{I}$ is the exact substring indicator function.
    \item \textbf{L2 Deep Memory (Micro-Chunk Tier):} Stores 512-token text passages augmented with Parent-Child context references and exact physical PDF page mappings:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Chunk}_{\text{L2}} = \langle \text{ChunkID}, \text{ParentID}, \text{DocTitle}, \text{PageNumber}, \vec{v}_{\text{dense}}, T_{\text{sparse}} \rangle
    \label{eq:chunk_l2}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Parallel Hybrid Retrieval and Dynamic Confident-Skip Reranking (DCSR)}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}When retrieving granular evidence at the L2 tier, Acamind executes the Parallel Hybrid Retrieval workflow (Fig.~\ref{fig:hybrid_retrieval}).

Upon receiving an empirical query $q$, the retrieval tier concurrently executes two search threads over PostgreSQL:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dense Semantic Search:} Computes cosine distance over 768-dimensional embeddings using pgvector HNSW index:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Sim}_{\text{dense}}(q, d) = 1 - \frac{E(q) \cdot E(d)}{\|E(q)\| \|E(d)\|}
    \label{eq:simdense}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Sparse Lexical Search:} Queries inverted index via GIN Full-Text Search implementing BM25:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Score}_{\text{BM25}}(q, d) = \sum_{t \in q} \text{IDF}(t) \cdot \frac{f(t, d)(k_1 + 1)}{f(t, d) + k_1\left(1 - b + b \frac{|d|}{\text{avgdl}}\right)}
    \label{eq:scorebm25}
    \end{equation}
    with parameters $k_1 = 1.2$ and $b = 0.75$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\columnwidth, height=5.8cm, keepaspectratio]{Figure4_Hybrid_Retrieval_Pipeline.png}
\caption{Block diagram of the Parallel Hybrid Retrieval workflow integrating concurrent HNSW Cosine and GIN FTS search, RRF rank fusion, and confidence evaluation.}
\label{fig:hybrid_retrieval}
\end{figure}

The ranked candidate sets from both threads are fused using Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) with constant $k=60$:
\begin{equation}
\text{RRF}(d) = \sum_{m \in \{\text{dense}, \text{sparse}\}} \frac{1}{60 + \text{rank}_m(d)}
\label{eq:rrf}
\end{equation}

To avoid redundant cross-encoder computation, the DCSR algorithm evaluates retrieval confidence $\text{Conf}(q, \mathcal{C})$ and top candidate score margin $\Delta_{\text{RRF}}$:
\begin{equation}
\text{Conf}(q, \mathcal{C}) = \min\left(1.0, 7.5 \cdot \text{RRF}_{\text{top1}} + \frac{0.5}{1 + e^{-20(\text{RRF}_{\text{top1}} - 0.015)}}\right)
\label{eq:conf}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{RRF}} = \frac{\text{RRF}(c_{(1)})}{\max(\text{RRF}(c_{(2)}), 10^{-6})}
\label{eq:deltarrf}
\end{equation}

\textbf{DCSR Decision Rule:} If $\text{Conf}(q, \mathcal{C}) \ge \tau_{\text{conf}}$ (0.75) and $\Delta_{\text{RRF}} \ge \tau_{\text{margin}}$ (1.20), the top candidate demonstrates unambiguous semantic dominance. The system bypasses heavy cross-encoder reranking entirely, passing retrieved contexts directly to generation in 0.08~ms. Otherwise, full cross-encoder reranking is executed to resolve candidate ambiguity.

\section{Experiments and Results}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Benchmark Corpus}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}All experiments were conducted on an on-premise benchmark workstation equipped with an Intel Core i7-11800H CPU @ 2.30 GHz (8 cores, 16 threads), 16.0 GB DDR4 RAM, running PostgreSQL 16.2 with pgvector extension on Windows 11 / WSL2. Embedding generation was performed using \texttt{intfloat/multilingual-e5-base} (768 dimensions), and generative synthesis was driven by Google Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite via live API execution.

\textbf{Benchmark Corpus:} The experimental corpus comprises 500 peer-reviewed clinical psychology papers downloaded from open scientific repositories (totaling 1,607.72 MB, 12,411 physical pages, and 70,794 micro-chunks). The benchmark query suite consists of 50 comprehensive research questions categorized into four distinct operational scenarios:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Scenario 1: Factual Clinical Q\&A} (20 queries)---Targeting specific intervention protocols, diagnostic criteria, and clinical trial parameters.
    \item \textit{Scenario 2: Cross-Article Comparison} (15 queries)---Requiring simultaneous comparative analysis across two or more independent intervention studies.
    \item \textit{Scenario 3: Literature Synthesis} (10 queries)---Aggregating thematic evidence across multiple publications within a clinical subfield.
    \item \textit{Scenario 4: Safe Refusal / Edge Testing} (5 queries)---Testing system resilience against out-of-domain queries, deceptive premises, and ungrounded assertions.
\end{itemize}
To guarantee rigorous academic objectivity, the reference answers, target clinical assessment scales, and physical PDF page ground-truth coordinates across all 50 queries were independently cross-validated by two domain researchers in clinical psychology, achieving high inter-rater reliability.

\textbf{Baseline Architectures:} Acamind is evaluated against two rigorously controlled on-premise baseline architectures running on the identical PostgreSQL testbed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Naive Dense RAG:} Pure dense vector search using pgvector HNSW index with Cosine distance.
    \item \textbf{BM25 Lexical RAG:} Pure keyword search using PostgreSQL GIN Full-Text Search.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Academic Evaluation Metrics}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}System performance is evaluated across eight quantitative dimensions:
(1) Database Retrieval Latency (P50 and Mean, in ms);
(2) LLM Generation Latency (Mean, in s);
(3) End-to-End Latency (P50 and P90, in s);
(4) Factual Faithfulness ($[0, 1]$ via RAGAS \cite{es2024ragas});
(5) Page-Level Citation Accuracy (\%), verified against physical PDF pages;
(6) DCSR Confident-Skip Rate (\%);
(7) Safe Refusal Rate (\%); and
(8) Statistical Hypothesis Testing via the Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($\alpha = 0.05$).

\textbf{Statistical Hypothesis Testing:} To verify that Acamind's improvements are statistically significant and not artifacts of sample variance, we conducted paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests ($\alpha = 0.05$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Citation Accuracy:} Acamind significantly outperforms BM25 ($W = 28.0, p = 0.0156 < 0.05$) and Naive Dense ($W = 21.0, p = 0.0312 < 0.05$). This rejects the null hypothesis $H_0$, proving that hierarchical L1/L2 chunking and parent-child context enrichment provide genuine advantages in precision grounding.
    \item \textbf{Factual Faithfulness:} Acamind achieves statistically significant improvement over BM25 ($p = 0.0245 < 0.05$) and maintains superior grounding over Naive Dense on complex cross-article queries ($p = 0.0428 < 0.05$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Discussion and Limitations}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}\textbf{Practical Clinical Value:} Acamind provides an accessible, cost-effective infrastructure for healthcare organizations and research groups operating under strict data governance regulations (e.g., HIPAA, GDPR). By self-hosting the retrieval database on standard hardware, clinical teams can synthesize proprietary or pre-publication trials without exposing sensitive data to external SaaS providers.

\textbf{Limitations:} We identify three primary limitations: (1) Ingestion scope: To optimize embedding throughput on standard CPU hardware, the current benchmark indexed representative sections (Title, Abstract, Methodology, and Findings). Queries demanding granular data buried in interior data tables occasionally triggered cautious safe refusals; (2) Multi-modal extraction: Complex clinical flowcharts and neuroimaging figures currently require multi-modal visual LLMs; and (3) Clinical translation: While extensively evaluated on published trial literature, validation within live electronic health record (EHR) workflows remains an imperative future milestone.

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}

\subsection{Concluding Remarks}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}This paper presented Acamind, a specialized Modular RAG architecture addressing the three fundamental limitations of clinical literature synthesis: document ingestion vulnerability, cross-encoder reranking latency, and lack of verifiable multi-document comparative structures. Through an automated Scan Circuit Breaker, a hierarchical Dual-Memory Knowledge Graph, and Parallel Hybrid Retrieval governed by Dynamic Confident-Skip Reranking (DCSR), Acamind establishes a robust and reproducible framework for clinical question answering. Rigorous empirical evaluation on 500 clinical psychology papers confirmed a median retrieval latency of 247.2~ms, an end-to-end response time of 1.38~s, and a page-level citation accuracy of 47.8\% with 100\% safe refusal reliability.

\subsection{Future Research Directions}~\\
\hspace*{\parindent}Future work will focus on three key advancements: (1) Multilingual cross-lingual adaptation, particularly optimizing biomedical embeddings for low-resource clinical terminology; (2) Integration of dynamic temporal knowledge graphs to continuously integrate updated clinical practice guidelines; and (3) Randomized controlled trials with psychiatric clinicians to measure quantitative diagnostic decision-support efficiency in hospital workflows.
